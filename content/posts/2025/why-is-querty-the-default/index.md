I brought a Logitech Keys-2-Go with me on holiday, so I could actually try to do some writing while away. To be fair to the Keys-2-Go, it's a pretty nice little keyboard. It feels nice to type on, takes two button cells as batteries and is thinner than my ipad without forcing me to use one of those ugly, overpriced, or cheaply made keyboard folios.

## What I learned
### 1. What’s going on at work day-to-day doesn’t actually matter
It is surprising how easy it is to forget the stress of day to day work when you’re 10,000 km away. Who cares if management is making decisions you don’t understand? Who cares that the security team has decided to block Spotify? I guess Spotify poses a massive security risk, despite the fact that on a developer machine you can run any code you compile yourself, secure or not. Whatever, I’m 33,000 feet in the air right now typing a blog post. I don’t care. 

That's not the point of this article - but some people really need to hear this more often: it's not that important. You're not as irreplacable as you think, and things will be fine while you unwind. {{< todo "Add link to 'It doesn't matter that much' article" >}}

### 2. The standard QWERTY keyboard layout is an absolute fucking abomination

I use a Kinesis Advantage 360 as my daily driver. It is the King of Keyboards, with a split ortholinear layout, reprogrammable keys, thumb clusters and concave keywells to minimise stretching. I love this thing to bits, and if it were to break I would instantly replace it despite its impressive price tag. If you're on the fence about it but you're willing to spend the time with it an learn, I cannot recommend it highly enough.

When I work at the office I tend not to do much typing anyway, but I bring a ZSA Moonlander in my work bag. It’s also split ortho, it also has thumb clusters, it’s also reprogrammable, but it’s nowhere near as comfortable (or as heavy) as the Kinesis. Its stock key lighting also caused my PO to remark “Dude your keyboard looks like a unicorn, I love it”, which has to be the best compliment you can get.

The thing with split ortholinear keyboards is that they take a while to get used to. You’re forced to use the correct fingers for each key, and bad habits like using your left index finger to hit the C key are hard to unlearn. Unless you had touch typing drilled into you from a young age, you probably do this too. Go on, try it. I’ll wait.

“Ah, yes, but” my imaginary opponent (who serves as a structural device rather than embodying a real position) begins. “If you type properly on a normal layout you would just use your left middle finger for C.”

Really bro? Look at this shit. [PICTURE OF KEYBOARD LAYOUT WITH HANDS POINTING THE RIGHT WAY FOR WHERE THE KEYS ARE, EG BOTH COMING FROM THE RIGHT] In what universe is that a normal hand position? Seriously?

Look at how contorted my wrists are. I have a choice - either move my hands all over the keyboard, or bend my fingers in ways they were never meant to be bent.

## This is indefensible

How did we as a species decide “You know what, this is good enough! Yeah my pinkies hurt when I use symbols more than 3 times an hour and everyone at work has carpal tunnel syndrome, but this is fine.”

[This is fine dog meme]

It is most definitely _not_ fine. Allow to me to illustrate with a metaphor.

The entire world sleeps beds of nails. It’s tricky to get right, kids hurt themselves learning to get on the thing and even once you learn to use it properly, there’s a small chance you’re going to get hurt anyway. Then someone comes along who says “Hey guys, I figured out this better type of bed that uses like some springs and foam and shit and it’s way more comfortable”.

“Oh, look at Dave over there with his 'ergonomic' bed!“ People say. “It takes so much getting used to I went right back to nails” a reviewer writes. “Why bother with ergobeds when you have a bed of nails already?” another remarks.

THIS IS EXACTLY HOW PEOPLE TALK ABOUT PROPER KEYBOARDS. 

“Ben why do you have a split keyboard you don’t need it”, or my personal favourite “you don’t need to type you, can just prompt copilot to do it for you”.

No, you’re all wrong, standard keyboard layouts suck giant amounts of ass and despite measurable effects where they cause RSI and CTS, we still choose the bed of nails. It boggles the mind, especially when a superior alternative is so readily available.

## If and when I have kids, I’m giving them split ortho keyboards with their first computers

It doesn’t even have to be Kinesis - I’m not married to the brand, they just happen to be the best right now. Let’s see more keyboard manufacturers build something that good, please! I’m so here for it. Because I’m telling you, the dumpster fire that is the “standard” keyboard layout should be relegated with steam-powered cars and valve-based computers to the annuls of “insane tech that I can’t believe was actually used”.

This shouldn’t be the standard.
